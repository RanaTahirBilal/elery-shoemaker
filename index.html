About

Four and a half decades in the safest industry that never gets to relax

Rusty Shoemaker has spent his entire working life in nuclear power, and almost all of it asking the same question: what does it take for people to operate a plant safely, day after day, for decades?

The answer he has built a career around is not equipment. It is leadership. Since 1995 he has held a series of roles at the Institute of Nuclear Power Operations and the World Association of Nuclear Operators, most recently as a senior corporate team leader — work that puts him inside stations and boardrooms across the industry, evaluating performance, investigating events and developing the people who will run the next generation of plants.

He has been instrumental in fostering leadership in the safe and reliable operation of nuclear power throughout the world. Marquis Who’s Who

Before that came fifteen years of hands-on utility experience with Carolina Power & Light — the company that would become Progress Energy and then Duke Energy — from 1980 to 1995. Those years laid the operational foundation everything since has been built on, and they are why his guidance carries weight with the operators who receive it.

Alongside the work came the study. A physics degree, then mechanical engineering, then two graduate qualifications in organizational leadership taken while working full time, finishing with a doctorate in 2022. It is an unusual arc — from the physics of the core to the psychology of the control room — and it is precisely the arc the industry needs.
